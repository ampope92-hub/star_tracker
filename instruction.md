We replay recorded star tracker passes through a camera head on the GNC bench, and the attitude solver for them still has to be written. It goes in `/app/tracker` as Swift: every `.swift` file directly in that folder, standard library and `Glibc` only (the toolchain here and in the grader has no Foundation or Dispatch). The program takes no arguments. It reads the pass from the camera head at `CAMERA_HOST` and `CAMERA_PORT`, uses the star catalogue at the path in `STAR_CATALOG`, and prints one line per frame to stdout: the frame counter, then the attitude quaternion w x y z, separated by spaces, and nothing else. The link protocol, the frame format, the camera geometry, the catalogue and the quaternion convention are written up in `/app/tracker/README.md`.

The grader builds it with `swiftc -O -static-stdlib` and runs it from `/app/tracker` twice, each time against a camera head it starts itself. One run replays the pass you can reach now, on a different address and port with other link lengths. The other replays a pass you haven't seen, from another camera unit, reached by host name, on another part of the sky with a 60,000-star flight catalogue and from another orbit. A run is stopped at 120 seconds of wall time and may map at most 2 GiB of address space.

Every frame needs exactly one line, and every line has to parse. A frame is graded if at least six catalogue stars were detected in it, so frames taken while the Earth fills the field are not. The grader scales each quaternion to unit length and compares it with the true attitude. The pointing axis (camera +z) has to be within 6.5 arc seconds of the truth on at least 99% of the graded frames and within 60 arc seconds on all of them, and the roll about that axis within 3 arc minutes on every graded frame. Both runs have to pass.
