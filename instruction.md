We replay recorded star tracker passes through a camera head on the GNC bench, and the attitude solver for them still has to be written. It goes in `/app/tracker` as Swift: every `.swift` file directly in that folder as regular files, not links or subdirectories; the toolchain here and in the grader has no Foundation and no Dispatch module. It reads the pass from the camera head at `CAMERA_HOST` and `CAMERA_PORT`, uses the star catalogue at the path in `STAR_CATALOG`, and prints one line per frame to stdout: the frame counter, then the attitude quaternion w x y z, separated by spaces, and nothing else. The link, frame format, camera geometry, catalogue and quaternion convention are in `/app/tracker/README.md`.

The grader builds it with `swiftc -O -static-stdlib` and runs it from `/app/tracker` twice against a camera head it starts. The grader refuses to let it start any other program. One run replays the pass you can reach now, on a different address and port with other link lengths. The other replays a pass you haven't seen, from another camera unit, reached by host name, on another part of the sky with a 60,000-star flight catalogue and from another orbit. A run gets 120 seconds of wall time and at most 2 GiB of address space.

Every frame needs exactly one line, every line has to parse, and the run exits 0. A frame is graded if at least six catalogue stars were detected in it, so Earth-filled frames are not. The grader scales each quaternion to unit length and compares it with the true attitude. The pointing axis (camera +z) has to be within 6.5 arc seconds of the truth on at least 99% of the graded frames and within 60 arc seconds on all of them, and the roll about that axis within 3 arc minutes on every graded frame. Both runs have to pass.
